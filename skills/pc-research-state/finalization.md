# Post-closure finalization writing

Use this guide when recording post-merge research records or final project outcomes.
Apply the main skill's resolve, check, edit, and verify steps along with the evidence contract below.

## Requirements

1. **Context & brief:** Require the unique absolute research entry, confirmed scientific judgments, original issue objective, verified PR/SHA identities, specific runs/data/parameters, evidence, and pipeline receipt or confirmed skip reason. Return unresolved scientific decisions to the project coordinator or user.
2. **Serialization & isolation:** Serialize writing to the shared project research home across sessions. Begin only after prior writing completed and coordination is unambiguous. Resolve the shared research entry rather than copying state into ephemeral checkouts. Verify whole-workspace ignore and tracked-file handling under the existing convention. Keep raw evidence at source and live task progress in the task system.
3. **Structured delivery evidence:** Record findings with evaluated `head`, `status` (`completed` or `blocked`), `evidence`, `blockers`, `scope`, `checks`, `artifacts`, and `limitations`.
   - Include changed report paths, affected folder mappings, dependent reports pending verification or rerun, integrated results, evidence status, pending disagreements, and deferred updates.
   - Every requested update must be recorded or have an accepted deferral that does not violate acceptance.
   - Required delivery evidence gaps block finalization.
   - Unresolved scientific questions may remain explicitly recorded; preserve plans, observations, interpretations, and conclusions as distinct kinds of knowledge.
4. **Infrastructure deliverables:** A report is required even when the task delivered infrastructure, pipeline rules, or tooling without immediate empirical findings.

**Done:** All requested updates are accounted for, navigation and local-only storage are verified, and receipt limitations distinguish open research questions from missing delivery evidence.
