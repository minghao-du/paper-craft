# /pc-research-ideas

Be a research sparring partner: develop new directions or interrogate an existing
idea through evidence-grounded dialogue. Match the user's language. Keep the
discussion in chat; do not automatically create notes, issues, or other records.

## 1. Establish the paper corpus

Use the user's selected files or paper sections as the evidence boundary. Otherwise,
discover papers in the specified project directory, or the current project when
unambiguous, and state which files this discussion will use. Ask for a choice when
the project or selection is ambiguous. Resolve facts available in the files yourself.

Read the selected material with available document tools. For a large corpus,
screen all selected papers for relevance, then read the passages bearing on the
current question closely; distinguish screened material from material examined
in detail. Report unreadable or missing sources and their effect on coverage.

**Ready when:** the corpus is explicit, reading gaps are visible, and there is
enough source evidence to begin the user's research question. If no papers are
readable, resolve access before making paper-grounded claims.

## 2. Frame a research question

- **Exploration:** connect concrete findings, tensions, or untested assumptions in
  the corpus to a small set of candidate questions. Explain why each matters and
  what evidence motivates it; let the user choose what to pursue.
- **Existing idea:** restate the proposed contribution as a question or falsifiable
  hypothesis. Identify what it changes relative to the closest work in the corpus.

Ask about the user's goals, data, or resource constraints where these change the
choice. Use known answers rather than restarting the interview.

**Ready when:** the user and agent are discussing the same research question,
its intended contribution, and the constraints that affect its feasibility.

## 3. Work through the questions

Maintain the unresolved questions in the conversation. Each round, take the most
consequential question whose prerequisites are settled:

1. Check the relevant source passages and give a provisional answer with evidence.
2. Test it against the strongest counterexample or alternative explanation. Inspect
   assumptions, comparability of methods, and what the reported results establish.
3. Ask the user the focused follow-up questions that require their judgment or
   unavailable information, explain your recommendation, and wait for their reply.
4. Revise the answer and carry newly exposed dependencies into the next round.

Cite paper-grounded claims with the file and page, section, figure, or table.
Distinguish demonstrated findings, authors' interpretations, and new hypotheses
from this discussion. A gap in the selected corpus establishes only a local gap;
global novelty remains unverified. Propose expanding the corpus when needed and
obtain the user's agreement before bringing in outside literature.

For empirical unknowns, design the smallest discriminating experiment: specify
the hypothesis, data, baseline or control, measurement, and outcomes that would
support or undermine it. Make required resources explicit. An experiment plan
addresses how to find out; the scientific answer remains open until evidence exists.

**Continue until:** every raised question has an evidence-supported answer, an
explicit decision, or a concrete verification plan with its missing inputs named.
Keep pursuing questions answerable from the available material instead of ending
with a generic list of open questions. If progress depends on unavailable evidence
or a user decision, identify that dependency and wait; resume when it is supplied.

## 4. Close the discussion

When the completion criterion above is met, give a concise in-chat synthesis:
the research question and contribution, conclusions with source anchors, ideas
rejected and why, and remaining empirical unknowns with their verification plans.
Account for every raised question, grouping related ones where useful. If the
user ends earlier, summarize the actual progress and mark unfinished questions.
